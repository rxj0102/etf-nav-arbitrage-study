Prices of bond exchange-traded funds often drift from the value of the bonds they hold. Whether these gaps close quickly matters to investors trading at ETF prices and to arbitrageurs who close them. We study five large fixed income ETFs (HYG, LQD, JNK, TLT, AGG) with daily data from June 2021 to June 2026, net asset values simulated by a calibrated mean-reverting process. We flag days when the premium or discount is unusually wide relative to its trailing quarter and track the next twenty days. We identify 160 dislocations at the two-standard-deviation threshold. The typical gap normalizes within nine trading days, and 57 percent close fully within ten. A rule that buys discounts and sells premiums earns roughly zero after costs (Sharpe 0.01, 51 percent hit rate), as the placebo design predicts. The framework is a ready template for testing whether live ETF price gaps offer profitable mean reversion.
